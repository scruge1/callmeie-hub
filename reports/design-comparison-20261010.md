## Ranked gaps

### 1. The whole-page composition is still generic

Home's full frame has a large lifestyle photo, then a website screenshot block, then a receiver close-up, then three familiar full-width information/action bands. The two phone images relate by object/colour but not by a developing visual explanation. No designed sequence connects website work, call answering and the next step. The lower half reverts to the same plain row/band language that caused the 'basic' rejection.

**Needed:** design the entire page as one reference-informed composition before adding more isolated assets. Use actual website work, a composed call-handling visual and a crafted voice sample as complementary evidence. Give each section its own scale and purpose within the same grid/identity. Do not merely insert another bitmap into another image/text row. Prototype and review the full Home before rolling its system out.

### 2. The hero says two products, but the visual explains one

Desktop reads 'Websites and call answering for your business' over a nostalgic telephone/table scene. The contextual light, shadows and surfaces are a clear improvement over the floating receiver. It still resembles an interiors/product photograph and communicates neither website craft nor how the AI service helps. Mobile puts the whole photograph above a separate text block, which makes it an attractive banner rather than an integrated product explanation.

**Needed:** choose a meaningful composition that visibly connects the two service paths without crowding. Options to study include an actual website/work surface integrated into the same environment, or a short visual transition from website work to a call outcome. A supporting laptop/screen must use actual owned site imagery, not fabricated proof. Separate foreground/background layers can add depth only if framing, lighting, text space and the story are designed together. The current1536x1024 compressed assets are not ultra-HD output; do not claim otherwise.

### 3. The small controls are not yet designed to the same standard

The supplied captures retain basic filled/outline rectangles and line links. Those shapes are consistent with the house rules and need not become pills. However, the controls do not show distinctive material, alignment, states or interaction detail that ties them to the new visual scene. Gallery screenshot gradients plus pale 'View concept' rectangles feel like a default overlay treatment. The current voice-player surface is absent from these screenshots, so the explicit request for a slick custom player remains unassessed.

**Needed:** build a restrained shared control language with precise geometry, line weight, spacing, focus/hover/pressed states and useful transitions. Design the real player in the house identity: clear play/pause, track identity, progress/scrub, elapsed/duration and any genuinely supported control. A waveform must represent actual audio or be identified as decorative; no fake interactive capability. Keep privacy/recording text readable. Do not bake essential button text into an image.

### 4. Gallery structure is clearer but still a catalogue

Websites now gives real work a strong hero presence; Lab shares the parent identity rather than competing with it. This is a real improvement. Below, repeated screenshot/title/label rows remain the main experience. On mobile the next screenshot is frequently a similarly framed block with another generic action. The technical labels 'Typographic-First' and 'Luxury Minimal' are extra reading that do not immediately help a business choose. The strongest stated benchmark, Clancy, is not the hero example in this candidate.

**Needed:** feature a small number of deliberately art-directed work entries with a concise customer-use label, then let the full catalogue remain available. Mix image scales/section composition within an intentional grid rather than making every screenshot another identical card. Consider choosing the actual strongest representative work after visual comparison; do not assume Slaney is stronger just because it is first. Keep each demo's own distinct identity and clear concept status. Ensure the whole screenshot is visible where that is useful; avoid an overlay that obscures relevant detail.

### 5. Section rhythm and action priority need another editorial pass

The old Home showed plain product rows. Current Home replaces those with useful imagery, but 'Try a call', 'Built and supported by Adam' and 'Tell me about your business' still form three repeated closing bands. Their combined effect is a generic landing-page finish. The mobile layout is readable and appears unclipped in supplied frames, but it becomes a long succession of image, heading, paragraph, price and link.

**Needed:** establish one primary next action for each product beat and a single strong closing action. Integrate the founder/support fact into a visually designed trust detail rather than requiring another heavy section unless it earns the space. Keep current short plain copy; do not compensate for weak visuals with narration, counters or claims. Details and legal notices can remain ordinary document flow without making the whole page a document.

### 6. Sophisticated behaviour is not evidenced yet

These stills cannot prove the requested layered animation, forward/reverse continuity, refined playback or helpful hover/detail interactions. A static composed photo is an improvement, not evidence of3D interaction. The user's own history distinguishes 'looks good' from 'works professionally', including reverse scrolling and intermediate-state reading.

**Needed:** if a depth/scroll treatment is selected, inspect actual desktop/mobile recordings at slow and brisk speed, forward and reverse, paused intermediate frames and first loading. Content must remain useful without motion. Avoid adding forced scroll distance or an impressive transition that delays the contact action. Motion is conditional on explaining the service, not a requirement to add fullWebGL everywhere.
